# Mining Autonomy — START HERE

## Mission
Build an exhaustive, evidence-backed map of legal/ToS-compliant ways to earn money autonomously or near-autonomously, then later decide what to implement.

Primary priority: **server-native online automation** — software/bots/agents/nodes that can run continuously on a rented or owned server and earn from simple, automatable work with minimal human input.

Secondary priority: **all other passive or semi-passive income models**, including monetizing home-computer resources, hardware, capital, digital assets, and systems that require an initial build or investment.

This phase is research only. Do not build earning bots yet.

## Core user requirements
- Search broadly and repeatedly until fresh passes stop producing genuinely new earning options/categories.
- Work in medium-sized coherent stages; avoid one huge fragile run.
- If a stage is too small, make the next materially broader/deeper.
- Save progress after every stage so any new chat can resume exactly.
- User interaction can stay minimal: while research is unfinished, report only `в процессе`; at true completion report `завершено` unless the user asks a question.
- The user may interrupt or change scope at any time.

## Research ordering
### Tier A — server-native autonomous earning
Examples: compute providers, storage providers, network/relay nodes, AI-incentive networks, validators/indexers/orchestrators, legitimate API/job markets, resource marketplaces, autonomous services.

### Tier B — autonomous but not ordinary VPS/server-native
Examples: residential bandwidth sharing, home GPU/CPU sharing, spare disk, physical DePIN, device-based contribution.

### Tier C — capital-based passive/semi-passive
Examples: deposits, bonds, staking, lending, liquidity provision, dividend/REIT-like instruments, revenue-sharing assets. Risk must be explicit.

### Tier D — build-once digital income systems
Examples: micro-SaaS, paid APIs, affiliate/content assets, licensing, stock media, templates, print-on-demand, digital products, data products, automated storefronts.

### Tier E — adjacent / speculative / rejected
Track ideas that look relevant but are non-paying, violate ToS when automated, require fake activity, are scams, or have economics too weak to justify deployment. Rejected ideas stay documented to prevent rediscovery.

## Hard safety / integrity boundaries
Do not design or recommend:
- CAPTCHA bypass;
- fake clicks/views/engagement;
- ad fraud;
- spam;
- credential abuse;
- unauthorized scraping/access;
- multi-accounting where forbidden;
- evasion of KYC/geofencing/platform limits;
- botting human-only tasks contrary to ToS;
- theft of compute/bandwidth/electricity;
- malware, cryptojacking, or deceptive installs.

A platform may be catalogued even if it fails these criteria, but it must be marked rejected or restricted.

## Completion condition
Research is COMPLETE only after:
1. all major taxonomies have been explored;
2. candidate projects have been deduplicated and classified;
3. high-priority candidates have current primary-source validation;
4. broad discovery passes and niche discovery passes are repeated;
5. repeated control passes produce no new independent categories and negligible new viable projects;
6. remaining unknowns are clearly listed rather than guessed.

When this happens, set `STATUS.md` to `COMPLETE` and document final saturation checks in `RUN_LOG.md`.
